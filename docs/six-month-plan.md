# The six-month plan

August 2026 – January 2027. Every figure assumes a ₱179,829.18 monthly surplus
against ₱539,448 of household income and ₱359,618.82 of committed outgoings.

**Total to deploy: ₱1,408,975** — ₱1,078,975 of surplus plus ₱330,000 of
paluwagan landing inside the window, including the ₱150,000 slot received early.

## Why this order

Protection first, because term life is cheap and cannot be bought after the
event. Then the cards, because they are the only debt still actively accruing.
Then a real cash buffer, because without one the next emergency becomes the
next loan and undoes everything. Only then the settlement fund — which is
useless until it is large enough to make an offer worth accepting.

The collections debt is deliberately last. It has already defaulted; another
four months changes very little. Paying it in dribs and drabs while carrying no
buffer is how families end up back where they started.

## Do

- **Move the money on payday.** Not at month end. A surplus you have to remember
  to save is a surplus that gets spent.
- **Open three accounts.** Bills, settlements, savings. Income lands in one and
  is pushed out the same day.
- **Get every settlement in writing before paying.** A signed release naming the
  account and stating the balance is closed in full. No release, no payment.
- **Keep paying the three loans.** They have end dates; breaking them restarts
  the clock and adds penalties.
- **Keep the paluwagan running.** It is the only large money you have scheduled.
- **Give every payout a job before it lands.** Decide in advance, in writing,
  what the September ₱40,000 and the February ₱150,000 are for.
- **Check your remittance rate.** See the income section below — this is worth
  more than most of the cuts available to you.
- **Review this page on the first of each month.** Tick the month off in the app.

## Don't

- **Don't use the cards again once cleared.** Freeze them. The whole point of
  paying ₱292,400 is that it does not come back.
- **Don't pay collections from an account your income lands in.** Use a separate
  bank. Agencies that know where your salary arrives behave differently.
- **Don't borrow to pay a debt.** Not from an app, not from family, not a
  balance transfer. It converts a settled problem into a live one.
- **Don't buy VUL or any investment-linked policy.** It is heavily sold in the
  Philippines and bundles expensive insurance with mediocre investing. Buy plain
  term and keep the difference.
- **Don't start investing until the collections are settled.** No investment
  reliably returns what settling defaulted debt returns.
- **Don't give collectors your employer, your new bank, or your address.** You
  are negotiating, not reporting.
- **Don't accept a verbal balance.** They are frequently wrong and always
  unenforceable.
- **Don't buy the condo.** See `docs/housing-decision.md`.
- **Don't tell every agency you have ₱736,575.** Negotiate one account at a
  time, and let each one believe it is competing for a limited pot. It is.

## More income

Household income is ₱539,448 from six contracts, all freelance. Three things
are worth more than any expense cut still available:

**1. Check what the remittance is costing you.** You bill in USD and spend in
PHP. If you are losing 3% to fees and spread on $9,120 a month, that is roughly
₱16,000 a month — ₱194,000 a year — for doing nothing. Compare what actually
lands against the mid-market rate on the day. Wise and Payoneer are usually
cheaper than a bank wire or PayPal. This is the single highest-return hour on
this page.

**2. Raise rates on the contracts you already have.** A 10% increase across
₱539,448 is ₱53,945 a month with no new clients, no new hours, and no new
platform. Annual increases are normal and expected in this work; not asking is
the anomaly. Ask the smallest client first to practise.

**3. Grow your husband's share.** He is at $1,920 against your $7,200 — 21% of
the household. That is both a capacity gap and a concentration risk. One more
contract on his side is worth more than most things you can cut.

Beyond those:

- **Move from hourly to retainer.** A fixed monthly fee for a defined scope pays
  better than hours and makes income predictable, which matters more than usual
  when nothing is guaranteed.
- **Productise something you already do repeatedly.** A recurring deliverable
  sold as a fixed-price package earns more per hour than the same work billed by
  the hour.
- **You are already running manpower at ₱20,000 a month.** That is the seed of
  an agency. Subcontracting work you have won at a margin scales in a way that
  your own hours never will.
- **Offer an annual prepay discount.** 5% off for twelve months upfront converts
  a client into working capital.
- **Reduce the WFMO concentration.** It is 42% of household income. Losing it
  costs ₱147,875 a month overnight. Replacing part of it with two smaller
  contracts is worth doing even at slightly lower total pay.

## If the plan slips

**One contract ends.** Stop the settlement fund, keep the emergency fund and
insurance, hold the loans. Do not touch the paluwagan.

**A payout is late.** The plan absorbs it — only the settlement fund moves, and
it has no deadline.

**Something unexpected and large.** That is what the emergency fund is for from
November onwards. Before then, the settlement fund is the buffer; it is meant to
be raidable.

**The surplus turns out to be smaller.** Edit the amounts in the app. The Plan
tab flags any month whose allocations no longer match what is actually available.
